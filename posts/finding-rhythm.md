# Finding Rhythm in Stillness

*June 1, 2026*

Dance is often thought of as movement — the visible arc of a limb, the
punctuation of a jump, momentum gathering and releasing. But I've been
thinking lately about the pauses. The breath held before a phrase begins.
The suspended moment at the top of a lift.

Stillness is not absence. It's accumulation.

---

When I teach, I notice that students almost always want to *do* more. Fill
the silence. Arrive at the next thing before the last one has finished landing.
It takes time to learn that a body at rest is still communicating — that the
audience is still watching, that the space is still shaped by you even when
you're not moving through it.

> The pause is where the audience catches up to you.

I heard that from a choreographer I worked with years ago. I didn't fully
understand it then. Now I think I do.

---

Rhythm isn't just the beat — it's the ratio of sound to silence. Of motion
to stillness. A dancer who never rests is unreadable. There's no contrast,
no relief, no way to feel the weight of what just happened.

This is true off the floor, too. I've been thinking about the rhythm of a
day, a week, a year. The way we fill every gap without asking whether it
deserves to be filled.

I don't have a clean conclusion. Mostly I'm just practicing sitting with
the pause. Letting it exist without rushing past it.

It turns out that's harder than most of the steps.
