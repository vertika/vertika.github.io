# Notes from the Studio

*May 15, 2026*

May was a teaching month. I had five regular classes, two workshops, and one
private student who is learning to dance for the first time at forty-three.
Here are the things I keep coming back to.

---

## Beginners see things clearly

When you've been doing something for twenty years, you stop noticing the
assumptions buried inside it. My forty-three-year-old student asked me why
we always start from standing. I had no good answer. We tried starting from
the floor. It changed the whole class.

Beginners don't know what's impossible yet. That's a gift.

---

## Correction is a form of attention

I used to think that correcting someone — adjusting a hip, redirecting
a glance — might feel like criticism. I've realized it's the opposite.
To correct someone is to say: *I'm watching you closely enough to see this.
You're worth the attention.*

The dancers who grow fastest are usually the ones who want the most
feedback, not the least.

---

## The room has its own intelligence

This one is harder to explain. On a good day, a class stops feeling like
a series of exercises I'm guiding people through, and starts feeling like
something we're all listening to together. The room wants something. The
energy suggests a direction.

I don't always know how to invite that. But I'm learning to stop when I
notice it, and stay a while.

---

Next month I'm starting a new Saturday series focused on improvisation and
score-making. It'll be for all levels — I'm curious what happens when
experienced dancers and beginners work side by side without a technique
hierarchy to hide behind.

More notes to follow.
